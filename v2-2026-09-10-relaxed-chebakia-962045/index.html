01 · The gap

An ETF's price drifts from what it holds.

When the price runs above that value, the agents create new shares and sell them. When it falls below, they buy shares and redeem them. The gap is small, and it opens every day across thousands of funds.

02 · The swarm

Five to fifty thousand agents. One job each.

Signals come in at the top. Agents predict, decide, approve, execute and settle, one gate at a time. Anything that fails a gate leaves. Anything that settles is written to a ledger nobody can edit.

03 · The stack

Agents decide. Code executes.

Eight layers. Models and agents sit at the top and choose what to do. Routing, execution, connectivity and permissions run underneath as deterministic code. Custody never moves.

04 · Live

Ten sessions on NASDAQ and NYSE. Every one above ten basis points.

Real capital, in premium and discount markets, with every fill confirmed by the executing broker.

02The swarm5,000 – 50,000 agents · six gates

Six gates between a signal and a settlement.

No single agent sees the whole trade. Each one does its job and hands off. A proposal that fails any gate stops there and is logged. Only what passes all six reaches the market.

01

Signal

Market data, NAV, flows and borrow, read continuously.

02

Predict

Direction and size of the gap, and how long it will last.

03

Decide

Propose a create or a redeem, with size and venue.

04reject here

05

Route to the best venue and fill. Idempotent, so nothing runs twice.

06

Settle

Reconcile with the broker. Write the record to the ledger.

03The stackEight layers · one boundary

Agents at the top. Deterministic code underneath.

Layers one to four decide what to do. Layers five to eight do it, the same way every time. That boundary is what lets thousands of agents run at once without any of them becoming a black box.
