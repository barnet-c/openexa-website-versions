01 · The work

Not tasks. Lifecycles.

The most valuable work in any industry is a lifecycle: many parties, hard rules, structured data, exceptions, gates and an audit trail. Six industries are made of them. One infrastructure runs them.

02 · The workforce

Five to fifty thousand agents. One job each.

Signals come in at the top. Agents predict, decide, approve, execute and record, one gate at a time. Anything that fails a gate leaves. Anything that completes is written to a ledger nobody can edit.

03 · The system

Agents decide. Code executes.

Eight layers. Models and agents at the top choose what to do. Routing, execution, connectivity and permissions underneath run as deterministic code. Nothing an agent believes can move what it is not permitted to.

04 · In production

The first lifecycle is already running.

Ten consecutive runs on real markets, every one above the floor we set, every action confirmed by an independent counterparty. We chose the smallest, lowest-risk lifecycle we could find. That was the point.

02The lifecycle testSix traits · all of them

Six traits. All six, or it isn't ours.

These are the properties that make a process too costly for people and exactly right for a swarm of narrow, verifiable agents on a shared ledger. Each one maps to a layer of the system.

Multi-party

Many hands, each with its own state and timing. Agents hold the whole sequence at once.

Regulated

Rules checked on every action, not sampled after. Encoded as policy the council enforces.

Structured

Fixed formats, machine-readable by design, machine-actionable through the MCP server.

Exception-heavy

Breaks, rejects and late data. The cases that consume human hours. Narrow agents resolve them.

Gated

Value moves only when a defined gate is passed and someone, or something accountable, has signed.

Audit-bound

Every transition reconstructible and tamper-evident. Append-only, hash-chained, replayable.
